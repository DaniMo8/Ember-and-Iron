Art direction · handcrafted diorama

A little house.
A living world.

A weathered medieval workshop, full of real work. Every ingot has weight. Every blade carries its maker’s touch. Every person has somewhere to be.

Cutaway rooms retain their readable diorama composition, with a grittier and more realistic finish: smoke-marked stone, worn oak, worked iron, repaired leather and restrained firelight. The final production direction replaces toy-like proportions and rounded plastic surfaces with believable construction and adult characters.

Approved design baseline. This book defines the look, room layouts, interactions, item construction, characters and new battle system. Its interactive illustrations are planning diagrams. The three promises

Design decisions that carry through every section
01

Make craftsmanship visible.

The blade on the anvil becomes the blade on the shelf and the blade in Renn’s hand. Recipe, alloy, workmanship and magic each leave a different visual signature.

02

Let the room tell its story.

A cold furnace is waiting for fuel. A full rack pauses the next melt. A tired employee sits down. People and machinery illustrate the actual game state.

03

Keep decisions readable.

One current objective, one selected object, one clear next action. The world occupies the centre; compact controls live at its edges. A beautiful scene must still work on a phone.

Shape & surface

  • Final characters are about six-and-a-half to seven heads tall, with adult faces, practical clothing, credible hands and feet, and grounded working or fighting stances. The first slice still uses transitional articulated figures; its smaller heads are a proportion study, not the final character assets.
  • Timbers show grain, square joinery and chipped edges; masonry has irregular joints, soot near fire and dust on ledges. Large planes stay calm so small crafted details read.
  • Wood grain, seams, grinding scratches, oxidation and hammered dents have a believable scale. Wear follows contact and heat rather than covering every object with uniform noise. Sculpted detail is baked into economical game meshes.
  • Early tools are repaired and cared for. Poverty reads as limited equipment and mismatched furniture, not filth everywhere.
  • Armour has cloth underneath, straps and real attachment points. Weapons retain credible medieval silhouettes.

Camera & light

  • Rooms use a near-orthographic three-quarter view, roughly 35° downward. A front wall is removed like a model-house cutaway.
  • Default camera is stable. Inspecting an object gently reframes it; a reset always returns home. No compulsory camera orbit.
  • Contact shadows ground feet and props. Directional window light, weaker cool fill and local firelight establish depth; surfaces remain legible in shadow and metal keeps controlled reflections. High quality improves finish and fit, with subdued ornament and bounded enchantment light.
  • Arena uses a higher angle, about 50°, keeping both front and back lines visible.
  • No depth-of-field blur across working controls. Reduced motion removes camera travel, shake and ambient swaying.

Finish the work you are watching

The active craft rests on a dedicated inspection support. It progresses through rough stock, shaped blank, assembly and surface finish according to its saved work fraction. Wood, cloth and jewellery use the correct bench process; a tunic is never struck on an anvil.

Finishing uses the existing +20, +10, +5, +2, +1 sequence, with the actual extra duration and quality ceiling. A queued pass shows the expected change and cost before commitment. Unknown prefixes remain labelled “possible” until completion.

No forging minigame is required. Inspect or rotate the object while the paid queue continues. Cancelling returns the saved escrow, not a newly recalculated recipe price.

One appearance contract

Every item resolves to a versioned descriptor: recipe, variant, tier, alloy grade, quality, natural prefix, treatment, enchantment and strength, inherited family, deterministic cosmetic seed and attachment slot.

The forge inspector uses the detailed mesh. Shelves and fighters use lighter versions of that exact construction. Save files store item identity and appearance inputs; they do not store GPU objects or model files.

Old items receive a stable cosmetic seed derived from their saved ID. An asset update cannot reroll an affix. Replays retain their historical equipment descriptors, including the correct off hand.

Combat redesign · live, deterministic, watchable

Watch your work hold the line.

Fighters approach, brace, aim, strike and recover in a real 3D arena. A shield should catch a blow. A lost front line should visibly expose the people behind it.

A genuine rules change. The current engine resolves a whole event timeline when a match launches. The proposal replaces that with an advancing combat state. The 3D view presents each current tick; outcomes are not preselected for the animation.
Battle storyboard · illustrative events

Choose stages above to inspect the proposed flow. This is a spatial storyboard, not a simulated match or a balance result.

Prepare, then watch

Before launch, choose the rival, active roster, front/back positions and doctrine. Inspect opponent roles and relevant defences. The qualification strip shows five scoring wins and all three rival styles for each of three rungs; then the champion becomes available.

Gather & launch validates and snapshots the selected team, locks their match equipment and begins one bout. Equipment changes requested during a fight wait until settlement. There is no manual attack tapping.

Live controls: follow fighter, overview, compact combat log and effects intensity. Replays add pause, scrub and 0.5× / 1× / 2× playback. Watching speed never changes production time or grants more rewards.

Readable physical rules

Each fighter has a position, heading, range, target and attack phase. Movement is confined to navigable lanes; units approach until their attack is in range. A deterministic separation rule avoids body overlap without a physics engine deciding hits.

All living front-line opponents must be defeated before the rear can be targeted. Bow arcs and spell effects may pass overhead visually, but their target still obeys that gate. No accidental bypass through projectile collision or camera angle.

Incoming impacts display a brief contact cue. Blocks bring up the shield; misses pass beside the target. Defeated fighters yield or kneel, with grounded weight and recovery, keeping combat readable and non-graphic.

Offline execution, migration and paired attacks

The same combat core advances live and headlessly. Offline reconciliation settles a saved active bout, then only runs already authorised exhibitions within their existing schedule and limits. It cannot launch a champion, reroll a seed or settle a replay. Rendering a battle is never required for it to finish.

An active version 1 bout at migration finishes with its recorded version 1 result. Newly launched fights use version 2. Keep old replay data and an explicit presentation adapter; do not rewrite a historical win using the new movement rules.

For paired dagger animations, first resolve the existing aggregate attack and mitigation budget. Distribute that resolved result across visible contact events with a deterministic split; the second contact must not apply armour subtraction, an affix or a critical multiplier again. Preserve the remaining scheduled contact when saving mid-combination. A future independent off-hand attack system would be a separate balance change.

A lived-in workshop

People with purpose.

A hired employee should be recognisable at a glance, present where they work, and visibly taking the rest that keeps them effective.

One person, one place

The founder works in the Forge and appears at the Smith desk when idle. Named specialists have one logical location: their department, in transit or resting in the common room. Selecting their card offers “Follow” and opens their current room.

Do not show an employee simultaneously tending a furnace and drinking tea. The portrait in a management card is a representation; a second full actor is not. Cross-room movement can finish while the destination is unloaded.

Mining crews remain a separate workforce. Show several representatives at higher headcounts, with the actual assignment count in the panel; do not spawn hundreds of characters.

Work, fatigue & rest

Use existing stamina bands: 70–100 full effect, 35–69 reduced effect, 1–34 tired, zero no contribution. Tired characters slow their illustrative gestures and pause between tasks; the underlying effectiveness is still calculated once.

Managed shifts rest at 35 and resume at 90. Manual leave stays off until the player returns them. XP reflects actual work. Cards keep clear XP and stamina bars, current contribution and the next training benefit.

Work → break → rest → ready is a saved status, not an animation guess. Being off camera never prevents recovery.

Shared rules in every room

  • Room title and one objective remain visible. The objective names the first actionable blocker and links to its source room.
  • The Develop button shows purchasable upgrades and exact blockers. Optional branches are explicitly marked; an available Surveyor’s ledger never implies the Iron licence is available.
  • No permanent floating button labels across the scene. Hover, focus or tap highlights an object; its controls appear in the inspector. A textual station list offers the same actions.
  • Leather, wood and oil stay together as compact amounts and buy controls on Mine and Forge. Material trading remains in Open workings.
  • First-visit overview: purpose, three-step flow and one next action. It can be dismissed and reopened without pausing work.

Input & comfort

  • Minimum 44px touch targets. Dragging a camera or item never triggers a purchase on release. Buttons validate current resources.
  • Keyboard navigation reaches every station and action. Escape closes inspectors; closing a sheet restores focus.
  • Quality icons, status text and patterns accompany colour. Positive and negative equipment changes keep +/− signs and green/red styling.
  • Keep transparency, dimming and audio controls. Add low/medium/high scene quality, reduced motion, camera shake and spell intensity options.
  • Room changes dim briefly and crossfade music. Captions and numbers stay crisp; no bright full-screen flashes. Ambient audio ducks for important cues.

Blender → browser

Model to a consistent world scale; apply transforms and use a shared material library. Bake sculpted detail and procedural materials into exportable base-colour, roughness/metallic, normal and emission textures. Author lower-detail meshes deliberately.

Export GLB with named object sockets and skeletal clips. Rebuild small dynamic rune/heat effects in the renderer; do not expect an arbitrary Blender node graph to reproduce itself in the browser. Validate the exported asset, not just the source scene.

Use a locked Three.js release with its matching loaders and decoders. GLTFLoader supports glTF 2.0 assets and explicit optional compression loaders; the current WebGLRenderer requires WebGL 2. Loader documentation · Renderer documentation.

A small, separate rendering layer

Keep the economy and commands authoritative. A read-only scene adapter emits room state, item descriptors, actor assignments and work progress. Renderers consume these; clicking an object sends the same validated command used by its HTML button.

Mount one persistent canvas outside the UI region that currently replaces its markup. Reuse scene resources, update only changed transforms and release unused geometry/textures. Room transitions cannot recreate the whole renderer or detach a pressed button.

Suggested modules: scene host, room presenters, item appearance resolver, character controller, shopping visits and combat v2. Keep migrations versioned. Do not rewrite working save, production or offline systems merely to add graphics.

Non-negotiable regression checks

Economy & continuity

  • Paid queues, finishing passes and exact cancellation refunds survive reload and asset failures.
  • Offline work is credited once, within supply and the 24-hour allowance; the unread return popup survives reopening and appears on Continue.
  • Full output bins prevent another smelt from starting; committed parallel output is included. Allowed last-batch overflow remains explicit.
  • Team items stay protected and auto-equip only when valid. Contracts complete from qualifying warehouse items. Public stock fills lowest quality first.
  • All inventory moves, customer purchases and match rewards are atomic; an animation end event never creates payment.
  • First Legacy still requires the fifth champion and at least 72 credited hours. Late rooms and deeper recipes remain earned.

Combat, art & usability

  • Replay, live, hidden-tab, low-frame-rate and headless runs reach the same outcome with the same seed.
  • Front lines cannot be bypassed. Multiple impacts at one tick have a stable order. Reload during a projectile flight preserves it.
  • Dagger off-hand, two-hand restrictions, armour fit and all six gear slots render correctly for each compatible class.
  • Compare win rates and battle lengths for undergeared, appropriately geared and overgeared teams against the old system; repeat campaign simulations before claiming unchanged pacing.
  • Check 320, 390, 768 and 1440px layouts, keyboard flow, long names, empty/full shelves, tired staff and missing inputs.
  • Profile room changes, ten-minute battles/replays and repeated item inspections; memory must settle instead of growing indefinitely.
