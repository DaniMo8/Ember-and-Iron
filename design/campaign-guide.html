The intended rhythm

Slow progress, useful returns.

The first hour

Learn the material chain, make the first contracts and prepare a three-person team. Earn the production ledger and delivery clerk. Set ingot targets, then authorise rotating contract work. These are achievable opening investments; retirement is a distant objective.

A fifteen-minute visit

Read the return ledger, resolve an input or storage shortage, choose one improvement and review a rival. Make or equip a counter, launch a deliberate fight and set production before leaving. Overnight uses the same rules as active production.

Read the return ledger→Resolve the bottleneck→Forge & equip→Fight & learn→Prepare the next absence

The measured schedule uses one initial hour, followed by 15-minute visits at hours 2, 4 and 6. Later days have four visits at hours 0, 2, 4 and 6. The remaining time, including overnight, is offline.

Each rung requires five wins and all three rival styles. Bronze work cannot satisfy an iron or later accreditation. Repeat upgrade ranks open with material licences. Easy exhibition wins provide limited daily training; they cannot replace qualification.

Measured first career: an informed earned run reached the Crown at 100.08 elapsed hours, about 4.2 days and 5.5 active hours. A conservative approach took about 126 hours. These are tested policies, not guaranteed completion times. Legacy is absent before 72 credited hours and the fifth championship.

A dependable absence

What the house actually earned.

Twenty-four hours of work

The allowance covers a whole absence, including reloads and calculation chunks. Miners, ingot policies, catalogue production, deliveries, authorised exhibitions and research continue. No free income is added just for being away.

Rotating work skips materials whose production chain is still locked and favours supplied orders. One basic contract remains available to recover the business.

A visible ledger

On return, see credited time, pieces forged, contracts, mining, ingots, wins and losses. Revenue is split into contracts, town sales and arena purses. Purchases, material changes, overflow losses, discoveries and completed studies are also reported.

Supply buying uses your explicit budget and retains an operating reserve. Full bins still lose overflow. Champions, upgrades and research starts need your decision.

Layers that emerge

Knowledge has a history.

Work a discipline→Find an unusual method→Read the discovery→Fund a study→Keep the knowledge forever

Eleven authored discoveries depend on time and demonstrated work: mining, smelting, crafting, contracts, employees, championships and later generations. Three new enchanting suffixes change combat options. Nine studies require actual resources and take two to twenty hours; only one runs at once.

Discoveries, completed studies and their passive benefits survive retirement. Early low-tier smith experience tapers as the smith advances, encouraging material progression rather than endless basic-item farming.

Beyond the first Crown

Choose what the next maker inherits.

Crucible trials

Sixty increasingly difficult trials cycle through heavy defences, fast blades and fire pressure. Later generations open further depth. Victories award seals once per career depth; replaying an old fight never pays again. Seals fund studies or permanent attack and defence sigils with escalating prices.

Optional oaths

The Iron Oath strengthens enemy health and armour. The Ember Oath increases enemy damage and forge time. The Silent Oath removes exhibition purses and strengthens champions. Each adds retirement sparks; first completion also awards seals.

Retirement retains research, discoveries, seals, sigils, talents, furnishings, chronicle and a chosen stored heirloom. Current production, attributes, mastery, staff, gold and league progress reset. The successor checklist makes re-enabling ingot targets, rotating contracts and deliveries explicit.

Later careers have shorter accreditation, eventually reaching a twelve-hour floor. Their actual length still depends on setup, equipment, decisions and the chosen oath.

Original Blender environments

The rooms remember your investment.

Eight rooms each have five themed backgrounds: bare rock tunnels in the mine, a glowing foundry, a working hearth, a timber shop, a sandy arena, a private study, communal quarters and an ancestral hall. Individual room upgrades determine local development; later generations and research enable the grandest architecture. The game keeps this evolution unannounced.

Menu → Options adjusts overlay transparency and scenery dimming live. Readable, Balanced and Scenic presets keep text solid. Each room has an original instrumental theme and matching ambience, with separate master, music and sound effects volumes. Music fades between rooms and pauses when the game is hidden.

Evidence and limits

Tested without granting the victories.

The scheduled player uses ordinary commands and the same simulation as the game. Earned runs do not grant gold, items, levels or championship flags. Separate fixtures test late content and visual layouts; those prove rules and presentation, not natural affordability or long-term enjoyment.

All seven callings have opening probes. The longer review records actual bottlenecks, a repaired second-generation contract deadlock and the earned resumption. Read the report for the final timings and remaining uncertainties. The deepest Crucible and fifth-generation folios still need human campaigns.
